How far to trust it

The accompaniment figure comes round four times a bar. Where at least two of those four readings agree, the position is marked plain; where one stands alone it is marked unconfirmed and is the first thing to check against the score. 98 of 229 positions are corroborated this way.

Against twenty-two positions read by eye off the scan, the program gets 17 of 22 right-hand figures and 19 of 22 left-hand chords, 14 of 22 completely. Those bars are a test, never an input: nothing hand-entered reaches this page.

Reading the diagrams

A position is one group of the triplet figure: the three keys the right hand covers, any melody note struck with them, and whatever the left hand is holding at that moment. Consecutive groups of the same shape are shown once, with the bars they span.

Fingering follows the shape rather than the bar that follows it. The right thumb takes the lowest note of the figure; when a melody note sits above, the figure is held back to the fourth finger so the fifth is free for it. The left hand puts 5 on the bottom and 1 on top.

Note names follow the engraving's spelling, so a key marked B♯2 is the one you would otherwise call C3. Where the right-hand figure is written down in the bass clef, its shading sits low on the keyboard.
